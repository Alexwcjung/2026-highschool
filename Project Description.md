# Project Description

## 🎯 Teaching Context

This project was designed for Korean vocational high school students. Although the learners are high school students, many of them have English proficiency around the elementary to early middle school level. They often struggle with listening, pronunciation, basic vocabulary, and confidence in speaking English.

The classroom is a regular vocational high school English class where students use smartphones, tablets, or computers. Since students have different levels of vocabulary knowledge and speaking ability, the lesson needs to provide simple input, repeated practice, and individualized support.

One major challenge is that many students are not highly motivated by traditional grammar-based English lessons. Some students feel anxious when they are asked to speak English in front of others. Therefore, this project uses familiar content such as pop songs, school-life vocabulary, short expressions, and speech recognition-based speaking practice to make English learning more accessible and less stressful.

## 🎵 Lesson Purpose

This lesson teaches students to listen to English, understand simple expressions, and practice speaking basic words and sentences.

The lesson begins with a pop song activity to increase motivation and provide meaningful listening input. Students listen to the song with lyrics or captions, answer simple comprehension questions, learn key expressions, and complete a matching activity.

After the pop song activity, students move to the Daily English 400 section. They listen to school-life vocabulary, check which words they know or do not know well, and practice speaking selected words or simple sentences through the speaking quiz.

This lesson is meaningful because it connects English learning to students’ interests and daily lives. Instead of starting with difficult grammar explanations or long reading passages, the lesson begins with listening, meaning, and speaking. This helps low-proficiency learners build confidence and experience English as something they can understand and use.

## 🧠 Theoretical Background

This app is based on several language learning principles.

First, the lesson reflects the idea of **Comprehensible Input**. Students first listen to understandable English words, expressions, and song lyrics before moving to speaking practice. This helps them become familiar with English sounds in a low-pressure way.

Second, the app uses **Reading While Listening**. In the pop song activity, students listen to English while reading lyrics or captions. This helps learners connect spoken English with written forms and meaning.

Third, the vocabulary activities reflect **Form-Meaning-Use Vocabulary Learning**. Students do not only memorize the Korean meaning of a word. They listen to the word, check its meaning, pronounce it, and practice it in a simple sentence.

Fourth, the speaking activities are related to **speech recognition-based speaking practice**. Students say words or sentences into the app, check whether the app recognizes their speech, and try again if necessary. This supports self-paced pronunciation and speaking practice.

Finally, the app reflects a **communicative approach** because it focuses on useful expressions that students can use in real situations, such as “I need a pencil,” “I have a question,” or “I am a student.”

## 📱 App Purpose

I built this app to support low-proficiency English learners who need more opportunities to listen, repeat, speak, and receive feedback.

In a regular classroom, it is difficult for one teacher to listen to every student’s pronunciation and provide immediate feedback. The app helps solve this problem by giving students individual listening and speaking practice.

The app supports self-paced learning. Students can listen to words and expressions repeatedly, check meanings, speak into the microphone, and see whether their pronunciation is recognized. This allows students to practice without the pressure of speaking in front of the whole class.

The app is not intended to replace the teacher. Instead, it supports teacher-guided instruction by providing repeated input, speaking practice, and immediate feedback.

## 🛠️ App Design

The app is a customized Streamlit-based English learning app. It includes several learning modules, such as:

- world map quiz
- pop song learning activities
- Daily English 400 vocabulary practice
- word-level speaking quiz
- sentence-level speaking practice
- matching games
- short reflection or writing activities

The pop song module includes background learning, lyrics or captions, listening activities, comprehension questions, key expressions, sentence matching, and short reflection activities. This module helps students receive meaningful English input in an enjoyable and low-anxiety context.

The Daily English 400 module organizes vocabulary into familiar themes, such as school life, classroom activities, home and daily life, hobbies, weather, shopping, and food.

Learners interact with the app by clicking buttons, listening to audio, checking meanings, selecting answers, matching expressions, marking difficult words, and speaking into the microphone. Students who need basic practice can start with single words. Students who are ready for more challenge can practice simple sentences.

## 🏫 Classroom Use

In the 50-minute lesson, the app is used in three main ways.

First, it is used as a warm-up tool through a short world map quiz. This helps students focus and creates an active classroom atmosphere.

Second, it is used for pop song-based listening. Students choose a song, learn background information, listen with lyrics or captions, answer comprehension questions, and practice key expressions. This makes listening more meaningful and engaging.

Third, it is used for vocabulary and speaking practice. Students listen to school-life words, identify words they do not know well, and practice pronouncing selected words in the speaking quiz. Students who are more confident can also practice short sentences using the target vocabulary.

The app improves the lesson by giving students more chances to listen and speak individually. It also helps the teacher identify difficult words and provide focused feedback. Students who need more practice can repeat words, while students who are ready can move on to sentence-level speaking. In this way, the app supports adaptive and self-paced learning.

## ⚠️ Limitations

One current limitation is that speech recognition is not always perfectly accurate. Recognition results can be affected by pronunciation, background noise, microphone quality, or device settings. Because of this, the app’s recognition result should be used as a practice signal rather than as a perfect assessment of pronunciation.

The app supports both word-level and sentence-level practice, but students still need teacher guidance. The teacher should model pronunciation, explain meaning, help students who have difficulty using the microphone, and connect the practiced words and sentences to real communication.

## 🚀 Future Development

In the future, the app could be improved in several ways.

First, the speech recognition function could become more learner-friendly. It should be flexible enough to accept minor pronunciation differences while still preventing clearly wrong answers from being accepted.

Second, the app could save each student’s difficult words and sentences so that they can review them in later lessons. This would make the learning experience more personalized.

Third, more sentence-level speaking activities could be added. For example, after practicing a word such as `pencil`, students could practice sentences such as `I need a pencil` or `This is my pencil`.

Fourth, the app could include progress tracking so that students and the teacher can see which words and sentences have been practiced, which items were recognized successfully, and which items need more review.

Finally, more pop songs, short readings, school-life expressions, and job-related communication tasks could be added to make the app more useful for vocational high school students.

## ✅ Summary

This project supports low-proficiency vocational high school students through pop song listening, caption-supported comprehension, vocabulary learning, and speech recognition-based speaking practice. The app helps students listen, repeat, speak, check, and try again at their own pace. It also helps the teacher provide more focused guidance and feedback. Overall, the project aims to make English learning more interactive, personalized, and confidence-building.
