# Project Description

## 🎯 Teaching Context

This project was designed for Korean vocational high school students. Although the learners are high school students, many of them have English proficiency around the elementary to early middle school level. Many students have experienced significant learning gaps in English, especially in listening, reading, basic vocabulary, writing, and speaking.

The classroom is a regular vocational high school English class where students use smartphones, tablets, or computers. Since students have different levels of vocabulary knowledge, reading ability, and speaking confidence, the lesson needs to provide simple input, repeated practice, and individualized support.

Many students do not plan to enter university after graduation. Because of this, they often do not feel a strong need to study English through traditional school lessons. For many students, English is seen as difficult, distant, and not directly connected to their future lives or careers.

Another important challenge is affective difficulty. Most students have low confidence in English and feel anxious when they are asked to speak, read, or write in English. They may avoid participation because they are afraid of making mistakes or being compared with others.

Therefore, this project aims to make English learning more accessible, enjoyable, and voluntary. Instead of relying only on textbook-based grammar instruction, the project uses familiar and accessible content such as pop songs, short reading passages, daily vocabulary, word cards, guided writing, and speech recognition-based speaking activities. Through these app-supported activities, students can participate more actively, review and retry tasks at their own pace, and experience English as something they can understand, practice, and use.

---

## 🎵 Lesson Purpose

This lesson teaches low-proficiency vocational high school students to practice English through familiar and app-supported activities. The lesson connects listening, reading, writing, vocabulary learning, and speaking practice in one 50-minute class.

Students begin with a short world map quiz to activate interest. Then, they work with familiar pop songs by listening to the lyrics, answering comprehension questions, noticing grammar patterns, completing sentence matching, writing a short reflection, and reviewing key expressions.

After the pop song section, students complete a short reading activity, such as a passage about Cristiano Ronaldo. They read the passage, find key information, answer comprehension questions, complete sentence matching, write a short guided letter or response with Google Translation support, and review key expressions from the passage.

Finally, students practice daily vocabulary through word cards and speech recognition-based speaking activities. They listen to words, check meanings, say selected words aloud, and retry difficult items.

This lesson is meaningful because it helps students experience English as an interactive and achievable activity. The main purpose is not perfect accuracy, but active participation, repeated practice, confidence building, and learner autonomy.

---

## 📱 App Purpose

I built this app to support low-proficiency vocational high school students who need simple input, repeated practice, and individualized support.

In a regular classroom, it is difficult for one teacher to check every student’s listening, reading, writing, vocabulary, and speaking practice at the same time. Some students need more listening support, some need help with reading, some need writing support, and others need repeated speaking practice.

This app addresses these needs by giving students individual opportunities to listen, read, write, speak, check meanings, receive feedback, and retry tasks at their own pace. It is not intended to replace the teacher. Instead, it supports teacher-guided instruction by giving students more opportunities to practice English individually and confidently.

---

## 🛠️ App Design

The app is a customized Streamlit-based English learning app. It includes several learning modules:

- world map quiz;
- familiar pop song learning activities;
- short reading activities;
- Daily English 400 vocabulary cards;
- speech recognition-based speaking activities;
- sentence matching games;
- guided writing and reflection activities;
- self-check and review activities.

The pop song module uses familiar songs and includes lyrics and comprehension quizzes, grammar discovery, sentence matching, short reflection writing, and key expression learning. Students interact with the module by listening, reading lyrics, answering questions, noticing patterns, matching expressions, and writing short responses.

The reading module uses short, accessible passages about familiar people or topics, such as Cristiano Ronaldo, Son Heung-min, Jordan, and school-life themes. Students read the passage, answer comprehension questions, complete sentence matching, write a short letter or response with Google Translation support, and review key expressions.

The Daily English 400 and speaking cards section uses daily vocabulary, especially school-life words and useful expressions. Students listen to vocabulary, check meanings, review difficult words, speak into the microphone, and retry words through speech recognition-based speaking practice.

The app uses familiar content, short texts, vocabulary cards, audio support, quizzes, matching tasks, writing support, and speech recognition feedback. Learners interact with the app by clicking buttons, listening to audio, selecting answers, matching expressions, writing responses, checking meanings, and speaking aloud.

## 🏫 Classroom Use

In the 50-minute lesson, the app is used as a teacher-guided learning tool.

First, the class begins with a short world map quiz. This works as a warm-up activity and helps students become more engaged.

Second, students move to the pop song section. They listen to a familiar pop song, follow the lyrics, answer comprehension questions, notice a grammar pattern, complete sentence matching, write a short reflection, and review key expressions.

Third, students complete a short reading activity. For example, they read a passage about Cristiano Ronaldo, find key information, answer questions, complete sentence matching, write a short guided letter or response with translation support, and review key expressions.

Finally, students use the speaking cards game. They listen to daily vocabulary, check meanings, say selected words aloud, and retry difficult words through speech recognition-based speaking activities.

The app improves the lesson by increasing individual practice opportunities. It allows students to listen again, review difficult items, write with support, speak privately, and retry tasks at their own pace. It also helps the teacher identify common difficulties and provide more focused feedback.

## ⚠️ Limitations

One current limitation is that speech recognition is not always perfectly accurate. Recognition results can be affected by pronunciation, background noise, microphone quality, device settings, or browser permissions. Therefore, the app’s recognition result should be used as a practice signal rather than as a perfect assessment of pronunciation.

Another limitation is that some students may depend too much on translation support during writing activities. The teacher needs to guide students to check and revise translated sentences instead of simply copying them.

The app also requires teacher guidance. Students still need the teacher to model pronunciation, explain meanings, support reading and writing tasks, help with technical issues, and connect app-based practice to real communication.

---

## 🚀 Future Development

In the future, the app could be improved in several ways.

First, the speech recognition function could become more learner-friendly. It should be flexible enough to accept minor pronunciation differences while still preventing clearly wrong answers from being accepted.

Second, the app could save each student’s difficult words, expressions, reading results, and speaking attempts so that they can review them in later lessons.

Third, more sentence-level speaking activities could be added. For example, after practicing a word such as `pencil`, students could practice sentences such as `I need a pencil` or `This is my pencil`.

Fourth, the reading section could include more vocational high school-related topics, such as school life, job interviews, workplace safety, tools, machines, and customer service expressions.

Fifth, writing support could be improved by giving students sentence starters, model answers, and revision feedback for short letters or reflection responses.

Finally, the app could include progress tracking so that students and the teacher can see which songs, readings, vocabulary items, writing tasks, and speaking tasks have been practiced and which items need more review.

## ✅ Summary

This project supports low-proficiency vocational high school students through familiar pop songs, short reading passages, guided writing, daily vocabulary learning, and speech recognition-based speaking practice.

The app helps students listen, read, write, speak, check, and try again at their own pace. The lesson is teacher-guided and app-supported. The teacher introduces the topic, models language, monitors students, and gives feedback, while the app provides repeated input, interactive tasks, individualized practice, and immediate feedback.

Overall, the project aims to make English learning more accessible, interactive, voluntary, and confidence-building for vocational high school learners.
