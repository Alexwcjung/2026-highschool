# Project Description

## Teaching Context

This project was designed for Korean vocational high school students. Although the learners are high school students, many of them have English proficiency around the elementary to early middle school level. They often have difficulty understanding English by sound, pronouncing basic words, and using simple expressions with confidence.

The classroom environment is a regular English class in a vocational high school. Students use smartphones, tablets, or computers to access the customized English learning app. Because students have different levels of vocabulary knowledge and speaking confidence, the lesson needs to provide simple input, repeated practice, and individualized support.

One major challenge is that many students are not motivated by traditional grammar-based English lessons. They may feel anxious when asked to speak English in front of others. Therefore, this project focuses on familiar topics, pop songs, school-life vocabulary, and speech recognition-based speaking practice to make English learning more accessible and less stressful.

## Lesson Purpose

The lesson teaches students to listen to English, understand simple expressions, and practice speaking basic vocabulary. The lesson begins with a pop song activity to increase interest and provide meaningful listening input. Students listen to the song with lyrics or captions, answer comprehension questions, learn key expressions, and complete a matching activity.

After the pop song activity, students move to the Daily English 400 vocabulary section. In this part, they listen to school-life vocabulary, check which words they know or do not know well, and practice saying selected words through the speaking quiz.

This lesson is meaningful because it connects English learning to students’ interests and daily lives. Instead of starting with difficult grammar explanations or long reading passages, the lesson begins with listening, meaning, and speaking. This helps low-proficiency learners build confidence and experience English as something they can understand and use.

## App Purpose

I built this app to support low-proficiency English learners who need more opportunities to listen, repeat, speak, and receive feedback. In a regular classroom, it is difficult for one teacher to listen to every student’s pronunciation and provide immediate feedback. The app helps solve this problem by giving students individual listening and speaking practice.

The app addresses the need for self-paced and repeated practice. Students can listen to words and expressions as many times as they need. They can also speak into the app and check whether their pronunciation is recognized. This allows students to practice without the pressure of speaking in front of the whole class.

The app is not intended to replace the teacher. Instead, it supports teacher-guided instruction by providing repeated input, speaking practice, and immediate feedback.

## App Design

The app is a customized Streamlit-based English learning app. It includes several learning modules, such as a world map quiz, pop song learning activities, Daily English 400 vocabulary practice, and a speech recognition-based speaking quiz.

The pop song module includes background learning, lyrics or captions, listening activities, comprehension questions, key expressions, sentence matching, and short reflection activities. This module helps students receive meaningful English input in an enjoyable and low-anxiety context.

The Daily English 400 module organizes vocabulary into familiar themes, such as school life, classroom activities, home and daily life, hobbies, weather, shopping, and food. In this lesson, students use the school-life vocabulary section.

Learners interact with the app by clicking buttons, listening to audio, checking meanings, selecting answers, matching expressions, marking difficult words, and speaking into the microphone. The speaking quiz uses speech recognition so that students can compare their pronunciation with the target word and try again if needed.

## Classroom Use

In the 50-minute lesson, the app is used in three main ways.

First, it is used as a warm-up tool through a short world map quiz. This helps students focus and creates an active classroom atmosphere.

Second, it is used for pop song-based listening. Students choose a song, learn background information, listen with lyrics or captions, answer comprehension questions, and practice key expressions. This makes listening more meaningful and engaging.

Third, it is used for vocabulary speaking practice. Students listen to school-life words, identify words they do not know well, and practice pronouncing selected words in the speaking quiz. The app provides speech recognition results, and students can retry difficult words at their own pace.

The app improves the lesson by giving students more chances to listen and speak individually. It also helps the teacher identify difficult words and provide focused feedback. Students who need more practice can repeat words, while students who are ready can move more quickly to the speaking quiz. In this way, the app supports adaptive and self-paced learning.

## Limitations

There are several current limitations.

First, speech recognition is not always perfect. Sometimes the app may not recognize a student’s pronunciation even when the student is trying correctly. It may also be affected by background noise, microphone quality, or device settings.

Second, some students may need help using the microphone or allowing browser permissions, especially on mobile devices.

Third, the app can support repeated practice and feedback, but it cannot fully replace teacher feedback. The teacher still needs to guide the lesson, explain meanings, model pronunciation, and help students connect words to real communication.

Fourth, the current lesson focuses mainly on listening, vocabulary, and word-level speaking practice. More support may be needed to help students move from single words to longer sentences and conversations.

## Future Development

In the future, the app could be improved in several ways.

First, the speech recognition function could be made more flexible and learner-friendly. It should be tolerant of minor pronunciation errors while still preventing clearly wrong answers from being accepted.

Second, the app could save each student’s difficult words so that they can review them in later lessons. This would make the learning experience more personalized.

Third, more sentence-level speaking activities could be added. For example, after practicing a word such as `pencil`, students could practice a sentence such as `I need a pencil.` This would help connect vocabulary learning to real communication.

Fourth, the app could include progress tracking so that students and the teacher can see which words have been practiced, which words were recognized successfully, and which words need more review.

Finally, more pop songs, reading materials, and job-related communication tasks could be added to make the app more useful for vocational high school students. These future improvements would help the app better support adaptive, meaningful, and confidence-building English learning.
