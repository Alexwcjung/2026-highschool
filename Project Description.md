# Project Description

## 🎯 Teaching Context

This project was designed for Korean vocational high school students. Although the learners are high school students, many of them have English proficiency around the elementary to early middle school level. Many students have experienced significant learning gaps in English, especially in listening, reading, basic vocabulary, writing, and speaking.

In this context, many students do not plan to enter university after graduation. They therefore often do not feel a strong need to study English through traditional school lessons. For many students, English is seen as difficult, distant, and not directly connected to their future lives or careers.

Another important issue is affective difficulty. Most students have low confidence in English and feel anxious when they are asked to speak, read, or write in English. They may avoid participation because they are afraid of making mistakes or being compared with others.

Therefore, this project aims to make English learning more accessible, enjoyable, and voluntary. Instead of relying only on textbook-based grammar instruction, the project uses familiar and accessible content such as pop songs, short reading passages, daily vocabulary, word cards, guided writing, and speech recognition-based speaking activities. Through these app-supported activities, students can participate more actively, choose and retry tasks at their own pace, and experience English as something they can understand, practice, and use.

---

## 🎵 Lesson Purpose

This lesson aims to help low-proficiency vocational high school students practice English through familiar songs, short reading passages, guided writing, daily vocabulary, and speaking-based activities.

The lesson begins with a short world map quiz to activate students’ interest. Then, students move to familiar pop song activities. In the pop song section, students listen to a song, follow the lyrics, answer simple comprehension questions, notice useful grammar patterns, complete sentence matching, write a short reflection, and review key expressions.

After the pop song section, students complete a short reading activity, such as a passage about Cristiano Ronaldo. Students read the passage, answer comprehension questions, complete sentence matching, write a short guided letter or response with Google Translation support, and review key expressions from the passage.

Finally, students move to the speaking cards game. They listen to daily vocabulary, check meanings, say selected words aloud, and retry difficult words through speech recognition-based speaking activities.

This lesson is meaningful because it connects English learning to students’ interests, daily life, and familiar topics. Instead of relying only on grammar explanations or textbook reading, the lesson combines listening, reading, writing, vocabulary learning, and speaking practice in a teacher-guided and app-supported way.

## 🧠 Theoretical Background

This app is designed based on several language learning principles related to low-proficiency EFL learners’ listening, reading, vocabulary learning, writing, speaking practice, and learner autonomy.

### 1. The Natural Approach

This app is more closely aligned with the Natural Approach than with Comprehensible Input alone. While comprehensible input is an important part of the design, the app also emphasizes low-anxiety learning, meaning-focused understanding, and gradual movement toward production.

Students first encounter English through familiar pop songs, lyrics, captions, short reading passages, key expressions, and simple comprehension tasks. After they receive enough support, they move on to matching activities, guided writing, vocabulary practice, and speaking activities. This sequence reflects the Natural Approach because learners are not forced to produce language before they are ready. Instead, they receive meaningful input and are gradually guided toward written and spoken use.

### 2. Reading While Listening (RWL)

The app uses Reading While Listening (RWL), especially in the pop song section. Students listen to English while reading lyrics or captions. This helps learners connect spoken English with written forms and meaning.

For low-proficiency learners, lyrics and captions provide useful support because they make spoken input more visible and understandable. In this app, written support does not replace listening. Rather, it helps students follow the sound, notice words and expressions, and understand the overall meaning more clearly.

### 3. Inductive Grammar Learning

The pop song module includes inductive grammar learning. Instead of giving students a grammar rule first, the app presents several example sentences from the song or from similar everyday contexts. Students notice repeated expressions, compare sentence patterns, infer the common structure, and then check the rule.

For example, students may see repeated sentences such as `I've been losing sleep`, `I've been dreaming`, and `I've been practicing English`. Before the rule is directly explained, they are asked to notice the repeated expression and connect the form with the meaning of an action or state continuing from the recent past to the present.

This approach is suitable for low-proficiency learners because grammar is introduced through short, meaningful, and familiar examples rather than long abstract explanations. Grammar learning moves from noticing to rule formation, practice, and simple personal use.

### 4. Reading-Based Comprehension and Information Finding

The reading section provides short, accessible passages about familiar people or topics, such as Cristiano Ronaldo, Son Heung-min, Jordan, and school-life themes. These readings are designed to help students find key information in the text rather than simply translate every sentence.

For example, in the Ronaldo passage, students can find information about his birthplace, family, first club, later career, awards, and personal belief about talent and habits. This kind of task helps students practice reading for specific information, sentence meaning, and basic comprehension.

The reading section also includes comprehension questions, sentence matching, letter writing, and key expression review. These activities help students move from reading input to meaning checking, guided writing, and expression practice.

### 5. Communicative Language Teaching (CLT)

The app reflects some principles of Communicative Language Teaching (CLT). The learning content is not organized only around grammar points. Instead, it focuses on expressions, vocabulary, and tasks that students can connect to real situations.

Students practice useful expressions from songs, reading passages, and daily vocabulary. They also write short guided responses or letters and practice saying vocabulary aloud. These activities help students experience English as a tool for meaning-making and communication, not only as a subject to memorize.

Although the app does not fully implement CLT in every activity, it reflects CLT principles by focusing on meaningful input, useful expressions, guided production, and real-life vocabulary.

### 6. Form-Meaning-Use Vocabulary Learning

The app supports form-meaning-use vocabulary learning. Students do not only see English words. They listen to words, check meanings, review examples, and say the words aloud.

This helps students connect:

- the written form of a word;
- the sound of the word;
- the meaning of the word;
- simple use in context;
- spoken production through repeated practice.

This is important for low-proficiency learners because they may recognize a word visually but still have difficulty understanding it by sound or saying it aloud. The word card and speaking activities help students build a stronger connection between vocabulary knowledge and actual use.

### 7. ASR-Based Speaking Practice

The speaking activities use automatic speech recognition (ASR). Students say words or sentences into the microphone and check whether the app recognizes their speech. If the result is not successful, they can listen again and retry.

This does not mean that the app is only a pronunciation checker. The main purpose is speaking-based vocabulary learning. Students practice saying daily vocabulary aloud, connect words with sounds and meanings, and build confidence through repeated speaking attempts.

This is useful in a classroom where one teacher cannot immediately listen to every student. The app gives each student an opportunity to speak, receive a recognition result, and retry individually.

### 8. Self-Paced and App-Supported Learning

The app supports self-paced learning because students can choose, review, and retry activities at their own pace. They can listen again, check meanings, review key expressions, use translation support when writing, and retry speaking tasks.

This structure supports learner autonomy. Students are encouraged to take more responsibility for their own learning by noticing which words, expressions, or tasks they need to practice more. The teacher still plays an important role by guiding the lesson, modeling language, monitoring students, and giving targeted feedback.

## 📱 App Purpose

I built this app to support low-proficiency English learners who need more opportunities to listen, read, write, speak, review, and receive feedback.

In a regular classroom, it is difficult for one teacher to provide individualized support to every student at the same time. Some students need more listening support, some need help with reading, some need writing support, and others need repeated speaking practice. The app helps address this problem by giving students individual opportunities to practice at their own pace.

The app is not intended to replace the teacher. Instead, it supports teacher-guided instruction by providing repeated input, interactive tasks, guided writing support, speaking practice, and immediate feedback.

## 🛠️ App Design

The app is a customized Streamlit-based English learning app. It includes several learning modules, such as:

- world map quiz;
- familiar pop song learning activities;
- short reading activities;
- Daily English 400 vocabulary cards;
- speech recognition-based speaking activities;
- sentence matching games;
- guided writing and reflection activities;
- self-check and review activities.

The pop song module includes:

- lyrics and comprehension quiz;
- grammar discovery;
- sentence matching;
- short reflection writing;
- key expression learning.

This module helps students receive meaningful listening input, follow lyrics, answer simple questions, notice useful grammar patterns, match expressions with meanings, and write short responses in a low-anxiety context.

The reading module includes:

- short reading passages;
- comprehension questions;
- sentence matching;
- letter writing with Google Translation support;
- key expression learning.

This module helps students read accessible passages, find key information, check understanding, practice expressions from the text, and produce short guided writing.

The Daily English 400 and speaking cards section includes:

- daily vocabulary listening;
- word-card-based meaning checking;
- review of difficult words;
- speaking-based vocabulary practice;
- speech recognition-based retry activities.

This section helps students connect daily vocabulary with sounds, meanings, and spoken production.

## 🏫 50-Minute Lesson Plan

### 👩‍🎓 1. Learner Profile

The learners are Korean vocational high school students. Although they are high school students, their English proficiency is around the elementary to early middle school level. Many students have low confidence in English, especially in listening, reading, writing, vocabulary learning, and speaking.

Therefore, this lesson provides short, familiar, and highly supported English activities. The lesson uses pop songs, short reading passages, vocabulary cards, listening support, guided writing, and speech recognition-based speaking practice to help students experience English in an interactive and less stressful way.

---

### 🏫 2. Class Setting

- **⏰ Class length:** 50 minutes
- **🏫 Class type:** Vocational high school English class
- **🌱 Student level:** Low-proficiency EFL learners
- **📱 Main tool:** Customized Streamlit English learning app
- **👨‍🏫 Lesson format:** Teacher-guided, app-supported learning

The app is used to support repeated listening, short reading practice, comprehension checking, vocabulary learning, guided writing, speaking practice, and self-paced review.

---

### 🎯 3. Lesson Topic

**Integrated English Learning through Familiar Pop Songs, Short Reading, Vocabulary Cards, and Speaking Practice**

Students begin with a simple world map quiz as a warm-up. Then, they learn English through familiar pop songs that can lower their anxiety and increase their interest. After that, they read a short, accessible passage, such as a story about Ronaldo, Son Heung-min, or Jordan. Finally, they learn and practice daily vocabulary through word cards, repeated listening, and speaking-based activities.

---

### ✅ 4. Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

1. 🌱 use the app more independently by choosing, reviewing, and retrying activities at their own pace.
2. 🎧 listen to familiar pop songs, follow the lyrics, answer simple comprehension questions, and notice useful expressions or grammar patterns.
3. 📖 read short, accessible passages and find key information in the text.
4. ✍️ write short guided responses or letters with teacher and translation support.
5. 🎤 practice saying daily vocabulary aloud through word cards and speech recognition-based speaking activities.

---

### 🗣️ 5. Target Language

- **🎵 Key expressions from familiar pop songs**  
  Examples: *Let it go.*, *Stand by me.*, *I need you.*, *I’m sorry.*

- **🔍 Simple grammar patterns from lyrics**  
  Examples: *I’m + adjective*, *I want to + verb*, *I’ve been + -ing*

- **📖 Simple reading expressions from the Ronaldo passage**  
  Examples: *was born in Portugal*, *grew up on the island*, *played for Sporting CP*, *moved to Manchester United*, *won Euro 2016*

- **📚 Daily vocabulary and speaking frames**  
  Examples: *school, teacher, homework, question, answer* / *I need a pencil.*, *I have a question.*

### 📱 7. App Integration

The customized app supports the lesson through:

- 🌍 a world map quiz for warm-up and engagement;
- 🎵 familiar pop song activities, including lyrics, comprehension quizzes, grammar discovery, sentence matching, reflection writing, and key expression learning;
- 📖 short reading activities, including passages, comprehension questions, sentence matching, letter writing, and key expression review;

- 🎤 speaking-based vocabulary practice using speech recognition;
- 🔁 self-check and review activities for repeated practice.

---

### 🧾 8. Feedback and Assessment

Assessment is formative and process-oriented. Rather than evaluating students only by final scores, the lesson focuses on students’ participation, task completion, repeated attempts, and gradual improvement across listening, reading, writing, vocabulary, and speaking activities.

Feedback is provided through multiple channels:

- 🌍 **Quiz-based feedback:** Students receive immediate results from the world map quiz, pop song comprehension quiz, and reading comprehension questions.
- 🔍 **Language-awareness feedback:** In the grammar discovery activity, students notice useful patterns from lyrics and receive teacher guidance to understand how the patterns work in context.
- 🧩 **Matching-task feedback:** Sentence matching activities help students check whether they understand the connection between English expressions and Korean meanings.
- 📖 **Reading-task feedback:** Reading comprehension, sentence matching, and key expression review help students monitor their understanding of the passage.
- ✍️ **Writing feedback:** In guided writing activities, students may use Google Translation support to draft or revise sentences, while the teacher helps them check meaning, clarity, and simple sentence accuracy.
- 📚 **Vocabulary feedback:** Word cards help students check meanings, listen repeatedly, and notice which daily vocabulary items need more review.
- 🎤 **Speaking feedback:** Speech recognition-based speaking activities give students immediate feedback on whether their spoken words are recognized, encouraging repeated speaking attempts.
- 👨‍🏫 **Teacher feedback:** The teacher observes students’ difficulties and provides short, targeted feedback on common problems in comprehension, vocabulary use, writing, and speaking.

The main goal of assessment is not perfect accuracy. The lesson emphasizes active participation, repeated exposure, self-checking, revision, retrying, and confidence building.

## ⚠️ Limitations

One current limitation is that speech recognition is not always perfectly accurate. Recognition results can be affected by pronunciation, background noise, microphone quality, device settings, or browser permissions. Because of this, the app’s recognition result should be used as a practice signal rather than as a perfect assessment of pronunciation.

Another limitation is that some students may depend too much on translation support during writing activities. Therefore, the teacher needs to guide students to check and revise translated sentences rather than simply copy them.

The app also requires teacher guidance. The teacher should model pronunciation, explain meanings, support reading and writing tasks, help students who have difficulty using the microphone, and connect app-based practice to real communication.

---

## 🚀 Future Development

In the future, the app could be improved in several ways.

First, the speech recognition function could become more learner-friendly. It should be flexible enough to accept minor pronunciation differences while still preventing clearly wrong answers from being accepted.

Second, the app could save each student’s difficult words, expressions, and reading results so that they can review them in later lessons. This would make the learning experience more personalized.

Third, more sentence-level speaking activities could be added. For example, after practicing a word such as `pencil`, students could practice sentences such as `I need a pencil` or `This is my pencil`.

Fourth, the reading section could include more vocational high school-related topics, such as school life, job interviews, workplace safety, tools, machines, and customer service expressions.

Fifth, writing support could be improved by giving students sentence starters, model answers, and revision feedback for short letters or reflection responses.

Finally, the app could include progress tracking so that students and the teacher can see which songs, readings, vocabulary items, and speaking tasks have been practiced and which items need more review.

## ✅ Summary

This project supports low-proficiency vocational high school students through familiar pop songs, short reading passages, guided writing, daily vocabulary learning, and speech recognition-based speaking practice. The app helps students listen, read, write, speak, check, and try again at their own pace.

The lesson is designed to be teacher-guided and app-supported. The teacher introduces the topic, models language, monitors students, and gives targeted feedback, while the app provides repeated input, interactive tasks, individualized practice, and immediate feedback.

Overall, the project aims to make English learning more accessible, interactive, personalized, and confidence-building for vocational high school learners.
